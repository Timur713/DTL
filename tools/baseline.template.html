BASELINE / NO MANEUVERS

40 aircraft · 20 minutes · explicit assumptions

Resolve the conflicts. Prove what can be proved. Show the trade-offs.

A verified vertical rerouting plan keeps all aircraft moving along their original horizontal routes. Two endpoint metrics attain mathematical lower bounds; temporary maneuver costs remain an optimization trade-off.

1. A result stronger than a search score

At 1200 s, two disjoint pairs are still horizontally too close on their original routes. Each needs 300 m relative vertical displacement. The triangle inequality requires at least 600 m total endpoint deviation and 150 m maximum individual deviation. Splitting each pair's displacement ±150 m attains both bounds in a continuously verified plan.

* Optimal only for these two endpoint metrics, this scenario, and fixed horizontal velocities. No global maneuver optimum is claimed.

2. Explain the cost of the model

The next modeling extension would compare horizontal reroutes with these unavoidable vertical costs, rather than merely increasing the same search budget.

3. Expose the limit of the guarantee

Exact states and threshold equality are modeling choices. Nominal safety is not a real-world robustness certificate; airport arrival and post-horizon flight are outside scope.
